# Explainability Layer (Milestone 8)

The Dream11 optimization pipeline includes a deterministic, leakage-safe explainability layer built using **SHAP** (SHapley Additive exPlanations).

## Why SHAP is Used
As we rely on complex Gradient Boosted Trees (LightGBM) to forecast points and play probabilities, understanding *why* the model makes a certain prediction is critical for trust and debugging. SHAP decomposes a prediction into the exact additive contribution of each feature, allowing us to see which variables pushed the prediction higher or lower compared to the baseline.

## What it Explains
The explainability layer generates local (per-player) and global (dataset-wide) explanations for the two core predictive models. It does this strictly downstream of the prediction step, ensuring that the explanations themselves cannot influence or leak data into the predictions.

### 1. FP Explanation
Explains the output of the Fantasy Points model (`E[FP | play]`). It answers: "If this player takes the field, why are they predicted to score X points?" Features highlighted here are typically related to recent form (EWMA of fantasy points), career strike rates, and situational bowling usage.

### 2. P(play) Explanation
Explains the output of the binary classification model predicting participation (`P(play)`). It answers: "Why does the model believe this player has a Y% chance of being in the playing XI?" Features highlighted here are often `days_since_last_match`, historical role stability, and squad rotation patterns.

## How Expected FP is Formed
The final value sent to the ILP optimizer is the **Expected Fantasy Points (Expected FP)**.
This is computed via a strict mathematical relationship:
```
Expected FP = P(play) × E[FP | play]
```
The SHAP text summary generated by the pipeline explicitly walks through both components, exposing the driving factors for the probability and the conditional points, then showing the final multiplied expectation.

## Limitations of SHAP
- **Correlation, Not Causation**: **SHAP explains model behavior, it does not prove causal relationships.** If SHAP shows that a low `economy_rate_career` strongly increases a bowler's predicted points, it simply means the model relies on that feature to output higher values; it does not guarantee that bowling economically *causes* more fantasy points.
- **Feature Dependence**: SHAP values distribute credit among correlated features. If two features provide the exact same information, their SHAP importance may be split, artificially making both look less important.
- **Latency Cost**: Tree SHAP calculation adds non-zero overhead (approx ~2-10ms per candidate). For strict real-time needs, `explain=False` can be passed to bypass explanation generation without affecting the core prediction.
